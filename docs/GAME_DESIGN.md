# Game design

Pirates of the Caribbean (2003) feel: free sailing on an open sea map, islands with ports, ship-to-ship combat, trading, RPG progression.

- **Sailing:** wind direction/strength drives speed; sail trim changes with point of sail; ships turn at a rate set by class.
- **Combat:** broadside cannons (left/right arcs, implemented in slice 5a), hull damage, boarding and crew (5b).
- **Economy:** ports buy/sell goods with price differences (implemented, see `ECONOMY.md`); ship purchase from the 16 types.
- **RPG:** captain levels and crew (implemented, below); skills, reputation and quests later.
- **Camera:** third-person orbit around the ship; tactical zoom out.

Planned in phases, see `ROADMAP.md`.

## Implemented: open-sea sailing (phase 3)
- **Controls:** `A`/`D` (or ←/→) rudder, `W`/`S` (or ↑/↓) one sail step (furled / half / full), `Q`/`E` port/starboard broadside, `G` aim zones, `Z`/`X` lay the guns lower/higher (range), `N` mute, `+`/`-` game speed (1×, 2×, 3×, 5×, 10×; it drops to 1× while an enemy is within 1.2 km, balls are in flight, you can board, are aground or sunk, and the HUD says why), `F` dock/undock, `B` board a crippled enemy alongside, `M` chart, `R` restart after sinking, mouse drag orbits the chase camera, `F3` debug overlay, `Esc` or the gear button opens the settings menu (the game pauses; "Galeriye dön" is a button there).
- **Wind:** slowly shifting direction and strength (default from the north, ~8 m/s). The HUD compass shows the wind relative to the bow.
- **Gusts and the wind a ship feels:** the wind is not the same everywhere. Bands of stronger and weaker air (0.75–1.25 × the wind's speed, averaging 1; `gustAt` in `world/wind.ts`, a pure function of place and time) drift downwind at about 5 m/s, so a ship meets lulls and gusts, and each ship (the enemies too) sails in the gust over it. A ship also feels the **apparent wind**: the true wind less its own motion (`apparentWind`): stronger and from further ahead sailing into the wind, weaker running before it. Heel comes from the apparent wind (less heel running downwind), and under sail a ship **slips to leeward** (leeway: about 4° at full heel, away from the wind, growing with heel and sail set; `SAILING.leeway`; the duel simulator has no leeway, so its tables are unchanged; the duel has no gusts either). The sails are trimmed to the apparent wind and the views carry it (`apparent` on the player, enemies and patrols, `gust` on the player). The drive still comes from the polar diagram on the true wind angle, so speeds on each point of sail are as before.
- **Point of sail:** drive depends on the angle to the wind (polar in `world/wind.ts`): 0 in irons, ~35% close-hauled, ~85% beam reach, best on a broad reach (~120°), ~80% dead downwind.
- **Speed:** top speed = original `speed` stat × 0.7 m/s, scaled by sail level, sail efficiency and wind strength (capped at 1.15×).
- **Turning:** `turn` stat × 0.006 rad/s at full rudder; the rudder works fully only with way on (full authority at 40% of top speed) but never drops below 35% while any sail is set, and a ship head to wind drifts slowly astern, so it can always fall off the wind.
- **Sails:** trimmed to the wind (yards braced, fore-and-aft sails sheeted out and mirrored to leeward, reefed by sail level); the hull heels away from the wind and rides the waves.

### Tuning reference (all in `gameplay/sailing.ts`, `world/wind.ts`, `render/*`)
- Wind: 8 ± 3 ± 1 m/s (about 4–12), direction swings about ±37° (periods 97 s and 31 s). Wind factor is clamped to 0.3–1.5 and speed is capped at 1.15× top, so wind above ~9 m/s adds nothing on a good point of sail.
- Easing: sail change 0.5/s, rudder 2/s, acceleration 0.35/s, drag 0.15/s (about 3 s up, 7 s down). Rudder use costs 5% speed per second at full lock.
- Heel: 0.14 rad × sail level × sin(wind angle) × wind factor; lean out of turns 0.06 rad.
- Waves: the sea state (0 glassy … 1 storm, from the wind plus rare storm cells, see `SEA.md`) scales amplitude 0.4–2.6×. Ships ride them as springs (`gameplay/buoyancy.ts`): heave follows the mean surface under the hull, pitch 80% and wave roll 60% of the slope, with natural periods that grow with length (12 m: heave ~3.8 s; 64 m: ~11.6 s), so small ships follow every crest and big ones swing slowly; a moored ship feels 30% of the swell.
- **Time of day:** a day lasts 24 minutes of play by default (menu: Gün uzunluğu, Zamanı dondur), the game starts at 09:00; the hour changes the light, the sky, the sea's colours and the ship lamps only, not how ships sail or fight. The weather simulator (menu: Hava & Zaman) takes over wind, sea state, wave size, cloud, haze, rain and lightning: wind and sea state do change handling, as they would in the game's own weather.
- **Sea state effects** (none below 0.3, full at 1; the duel simulator and `docs/BALANCE.md` assume fair weather): head seas cost up to 22% speed × a size factor (30 m / length, 0.5–1.6), turning up to 20%, ships with freeboard under ~2.1 m take green water over the bow (up to 0.02 flooding/s, pumps clear 0.003/s), and a rolling hull widens its broadside's scatter up to ~1.5×. Sea state comes from the wind plus rare storm cells (first after ~17 min, storms about a tenth of the time); the HUD shows it (Sakin … Fırtına), ambience gets louder, and `?sea=0…1` / `__game.dev.setSea(x)` force it.
- Camera: mouse drag and wheel orbit the ship (0.7–14× ship length); the camera swings with the ship's heading changes but is not pulled back behind it after a manual orbit.
- Names: `ShipView.knots` is the original game's speed unit, not real knots.
- Language: the in-game UI text is Turkish; docs and code are English. Localisation is not planned yet.
- Known simplifications: no tacking penalty, leeway or luffing in irons (sails brace but do not flap).

## Implemented: archipelago, ports, navigation (phase 4)
- **World:** 8 islands and the 11 ports of the original map (Redmond, Greenford, Oxbay, Falaise de Fleur, Conceicao, Conceicao Smugglers, Quebradas Costillas, Isla Muelle, Douwesen, Douwesen Pirates, Khoal Roa), laid out like the guide's map (north up, neighbours ~0.9 km apart, a minute or two of sailing). Islands are procedural (noisy cone + beach + peak) from `world/islands.ts`.
- **Run aground:** the hull is sampled at five points; any point over water shallower than `draft + 0.5 m` stops the ship, bounces it back stern-first for 1.5 s (sails useless, rudder works at half authority) and shows "Karaya oturdun!". Big keels cannot enter shoals, ports have a 9 m deep anchorage.
- **Docking:** inside the port's marked entry zone (a gold rectangle on the sea with corner posts and an anchor sign; the minimap and chart mark ports with anchors) and under 2.5 m/s, `F` moors the ship (it cannot move, sails come down) and opens the harbour panel; `F` again casts off. The harbour panel has a market and a tavern (`ECONOMY.md`); the shipyard sells and repairs ships (below).
- **Navigation:** HUD shows the nearest port's name, distance and bearing plus a marker on the compass ring; the minimap (always on) and the chart (`M`) show only discovered islands and ports (fog of war): an island is discovered within its radius × 1.3 + 350 m, a port town within 500 m. Start: just off Douwesen, facing open water.
- **Looks:** low-poly terrain with sand/grass/rock colouring, cone trees, houses, a pier, flag and a floating name label per port; the sea tints to lagoon green and foams in the shallows (depth texture baked from the same height function) and extends to a hazy horizon (a clip-map mesh, fine near the camera and coarser in each ring outward; each wave fades out where the mesh is too coarse to hold it).
- **Not yet:** landing on shore. Discovery persists in the saved game (5f).

## Implemented: broadsides and one enemy (phase 5a)
- **Guns:** half of the `cannons` stat per side (max 24 balls a volley; a bigger broadside makes each ball hit harder so total firepower is kept); `Q` fires the port side, `E` starboard. A broadside reloads in 6 s (player) / 8 s (enemy) and cannot be fired while moored. An unarmed ship (Tartane) simply cannot shoot.
- **Ballistics:** muzzle speed 92 m/s, gravity 9.81, quadratic air drag (0.00035 /m, against the air, so a wind pushes a ball a little sideways), ±2° sideways spread (×1.3 for the enemy), 4–5° elevation (the balls come down 140–170 m from a Brig's centreline, 130–200 m for the biggest ships); balls inherit the ship's speed, spread along the hull and fan out. Balls fly in three sub-steps a frame so they cannot skip through a narrow hull. A ball that comes down in the sea simply sinks: it splashes once, at the height of the wave surface it hits, not on a flat sea. A hit carries the hull side's outward normal and the ball's speed (for the effects). The aim aid tabulates the same flight (`trajectoryOf` in `combat.ts`), so it stays true to the drag.
- **Laying the guns (Z / X):** the player lays the guns lower (shorter range) or higher (longer range) while a key is held (0.06 rad/s, between 0.02 and 0.22 rad; 0.08 rad at rest, the middle of the old fixed band, so nothing changes if the keys are never touched). Each ball still scatters ±0.01 rad around the aimed elevation. The HUD shows the elevation in degrees and the range of the middle ball; each broadside draws its real flight path as a line of beads from the muzzle to a ring on the sea (with drag, the wind and the ship's own speed, from `arcFor`), and the green/gold landing zone moves with the elevation. Enemies keep the old random band, so the balance tables are unchanged.
- **A broadside ripples:** the guns do not fire together. Each gun waits 0 to about 0.9 s (fore to aft or aft to fore, each a little early or late, drawn from the game's seeded generator when the broadside is ordered), then fires from the ship as it is at that moment, with its own muzzle blast, and kicks the hull's roll away from the guns (bigger kick on small hulls, `gunScatter` widens the later guns on a rolling ship) and shakes the camera a little. A gun whose ship has sunk never fires. The duel simulator still fires a broadside at once.
- **Damage:** a hit costs `shot calibre × 2.5` hull points (12 lb = 30). A ball hits if it is inside the hull footprint, below keel+rail height (+1.5 m rigging). At 0 hull points the ship sinks over 8 s: the player sees "Gemin battı", `R` restarts after a 2 s pause (full hull, kills reset); an enemy is removed after sinking and a new one arrives 25 s after the kill (the timer starts at the kill) about 650 m away.
- **Enemy handicap:** the enemy has 80% of its type's hull points, reloads in 8 s and spreads its shots 1.3× wider. These values come from measured duels: see `docs/BALANCE.md`.
- **Enemy AI** (`gameplay/enemy.ts`): same sailing model as the player (wind, turning, grounding), fully rigged; it closes in, turns broadside at 130–300 m, fires when you are within ±20° of abeam and 180 m, stays out of the no-go zone (±43° of the wind) and turns away from shoals it sees 90 m ahead. Its type is chosen by `pickEnemySpec`: armed (8+ cannons), no more than 1.2× your speed, strength (√(hull × broadside damage)) within 0.45–1.7× of yours and closest to 0.85× of it; an unarmed ship is hunted by the weakest armed ship that cannot outrun it. See `docs/BALANCE.md`.
- **Fleeing and escape (5a.2a, rebalanced in 5a.4c):** an enemy below 25% of its hull breaks off for good (it runs straight away from you, bearing off the wind if it must, and does not fire); the HUD shows "(kaçıyor)". An enemy more than 1 km from you for 20 s gives up and is replaced after 25 s ("Düşman gemisi gözden kayboldu"), and a fleeing enemy that gets away is not a kill. Enemies are never more than 1.2× faster than you, so you are not hunted by something you cannot hold your own against, and outrunning one is a matter of being faster or finding better wind; a slower ship that cannot win can still dock (safe in port). The Tartane has no guns: its hunter is a weak Barque, no faster than it, and it must run or dock.
- **Several enemies (5a.2b):** one at first, two after 2 kills, three after 4. They are a mixed squadron (armed ships no more than 1.2× your speed and within 0.45–1.7× your strength, √(hull × broadside damage); the closest to 0.85× of it first), arrive 12 s apart (25 s after a kill or escape; a kill or escape resets the arrival timer, so a pending second enemy can wait up to 25 s) about 650 m away: the first try is dead ahead of you, then random bearings, never within 140 m of another enemy, and steer apart when within 70 m of each other. The HUD target is the nearest one afloat (shown out to 1 km, the give-up range); all show on the minimap.
- **Nations and flags (5a.2c):** `world/nations.ts` has six flags: free captain (you, cream), English (red), French (blue), Spanish (yellow), Dutch (orange) and pirate (black). Each ship flies its flag on the masthead pennant and on an ensign at the stern. Enemies take their nation in turn (pirate first); the HUD target shows a flag chip and e.g. "Korsan Schooner", and the minimap marks each enemy in its flag colour. Until factions (5e) every nation is hostile.
- **Repair and harbour:** a moored ship mends 0.2% of its hull per second (rigging, leaks and fire much faster; the shipyard repairs the hull at once for gold) and is safe: enemies neither fire at nor hit a docked ship (the escape for a ship that cannot win, e.g. the unarmed Tartane). Restart (`R`) pressed while the ship is still going down is honoured once the sunk screen shows.
- **Aim aid (5a.1a, corrected in 5a.4):** each broadside shows where it can hit, on the sea beside the ship (`G` toggles). It is computed by `aimZone` in `gameplay/combat.ts` from the same ballistics the balls use, and it is the stretch where a ball is airborne **and below a ship's hull-and-rigging height**, not just where the balls land: a ball flies low and flat over the first stretch, climbs, then falls, so low shots hit close in and high shots only hit near the far edge. For a Brig the zone runs from the hull out to about 170 m; its shading is the chance of a hit (`profile`, from ~100% close in, thinning toward the far edge because the high-angle shots pass over a target at middle distances), plus a span of 60% of the hull length, shifted forward by the ship's speed. Gold when loaded, green when an enemy lies inside (the HUD gun bar glows green too), grey while reloading, plus a line from the hull to the far edge. A test fires balls at a target at several distances and checks the measured hit rate matches the predicted chance. Two things to know when reading the shading: it models only the *vertical* chance (is the ball below the hull height there), not sideways scatter or how much of the target's side faces you, so a target seen end-on or moving diagonally is hit less often than shown (runtime QA measured ~29% at 40 m where the shading said 50–70%, and ~67% at 127 m where it said 100%); and the dip at about 60–75 m is real ballistics: most shots are at the top of their arc there and pass over a ship's hull and rigging height, while low shots hit close in and falling shots hit farther out. Zoom the camera out (wheel, up to 14 ship lengths) to see it whole.
- **Hit zones and rigging (5a.3a):** where a ball strikes decides what it damages (`ballHitZone`, height above the waterline): below 0.8 m *waterline*, below the deck line *hull*, up to 1.5 m above it *rigging*. Hull and waterline hits take the full damage. A rigging hit costs 30% of it in hull points and wrecks `damage ÷ max hull × 3` of the rigging (0..1), so small ships lose their sails faster: about 20 hits take a Brig to the floor. Rigging scales speed (down to 35% at 0) and turning (down to 55%) for the player and the enemy alike. It mends 0.15% a second at sea up to 70%, and 3% a second when moored (`gameplay/damage.ts`). The HUD shows an *Arma* bar, and the target panel the enemy's (plus 🔥 when it burns and 💧 when it is flooding). Hit events carry `zone`.
- **Leaks and fire (5a.3b/c):** a waterline hit also floods the ship by `damage ÷ max hull × 6` (0 dry … 1 swamped, 40 damage on a Brig ≈ 10%). Flooding drains 0.3% of the hull per second at 1 and slows the ship down to 55% of its speed; the crew pumps 0.3%/s at sea (a single hit clears in ~30 s) and 5%/s moored. A rigging hit has a 15% chance to start a fire, a hull hit 6% (waterline never): fire starts at 0.35 (+0.1 if already burning), drains 0.2% of the hull and burns 1% of the rigging per second at 1, the crew puts it out at 2%/s, and it is out in seconds in harbour. Both are carried on the ship (`flooding`, `fire`), apply to the enemy too (`strike`, `stepAffliction` in `gameplay/damage.ts`, shared with the duel simulator), and can sink a ship without a further hit. The HUD shows *Su* and *Yangın* rows (red while non-zero). Visuals (5a.3d): `sailingMode` calls `Ship.setCondition` for every ship each frame (torn, scorched sails, a ship low in the water and listing, flames), `sailingView` calls `effects.burn` for each burning ship (flames and smoke), hit particles and sounds depend on the zone, and `audio.condition` layers leak, fire and creak ambience for the player's ship.
- **Hit feedback (5a.1b):** every `hit` event carries the hull points taken off. One floating number per target per volley (merged within 20 m; gold when we hit them, red when they hit us, readable at any distance); a ✕ marker flashes when our shot lands; a red screen-edge flash and a camera shake (stronger with more damage) when we are hit.
- **Sound (5a.1c):** all synthesised with Web Audio, no files: cannon (noise crack over a deep thump, rolling spread across a broadside), hit (wood crack), splash, a sinking groan with bubbles, and looping sea, wind and hull-water layers whose loudness follows the wind and your speed. Positioned by `spatialMix`: gain falls with distance (half at 120 m), pan follows the bearing, and a sound arrives after distance ÷ 340 m/s (up to 2.5 s) so a far broadside is seen before it is heard. At most 10 voices start per frame. Starts on the first key press (browser rule); `N` mutes.
- **HUD:** hull bar, left/right reload bars, nearest enemy name/distance/bearing/hull, kill toast; the minimap shows enemy ships as triangles in their nation's flag colour.
- **Not yet:** formations, friend/foe by nation, a reward that tells a kill from an escape (a kill now pays experience, see below).

## Ship against ship (collisions)
Every ship afloat is an oriented box (0.92 of its length by 0.84 of its beam) with a mass from its displacement (`length × beam × draft × 0.5` tonnes). The player, the enemies and the patrols collide (`gameplay/collision.ts`, wired by `crashes.ts` after the enemies move): the overlap is resolved along the axis of least penetration, the ships trade an impulse (restitution 0.2) that changes each speed along the keel and swings the bow by the off-centre contact (at most 0.35 rad), and a crash that pushes a ship onto a shoal is undone. The patrols are hulls like any other (they take no damage and never collide with each other): a crash shoves one off its rail in proportion to how light it is, and it sails back onto its ellipse over about five seconds (`createNpcFleet` `nudge`). Hurt scales with the energy lost, shared by each hull's mass: **hull fraction lost = lost energy (kJ) / (hull mass (t) × 120)**, at most 30 % per contact, nothing below 1 m/s closing speed (a scrape); a blow above 3 m/s also opens the planking below the waterline (flooding). A brig ramming a brig at 6 m/s costs each about 7 %; a tartane rammed by a battleship comes off well over a hundred times worse in proportion to its hull (the 30 % cap per contact bites first). Simplifications: the keel resists sliding sideways, so only the part of an impulse along a ship's keel changes its speed (a broadside T-bone mostly moves and turns the struck ship), the bow swing is a fixed response rather than a simulated spin, and touches below the scrape speed make no event. A `collision` event (contact point, normal, closing speed, hull points lost) drives sparks, flying planks, a crunch sound and a small camera shake (`render/shake.ts`: at most ~0.2 m of camera movement for the hardest crash, a little per gun, more for hits on the player, all gone within about a tenth of a second). The duel simulator has no collisions, so the balance tables are unaffected.

## Gold, crew, plunder and levels (5b)
- **State:** the run's persistent data is a `GameState` (`gameplay/state.ts`, see `ARCHITECTURE.md`): gold (a new captain has 2 000), crew count, hold (goods in units of hold, capacity `stats.hold`), experience. The HUD panel shows gold, level with an experience bar, crew `n / max` (red below 75 %) and hold `used / capacity`.
- **Crew and guns:** crew starts at the ship's `stats.crew`. Reload time is `COMBAT.playerReload ÷ crewFactor × reloadBonus(level)`, where `crewFactor = clamp(crew ÷ full crew, 0.3, 1)`: **exactly 6 s at full crew and level 1**, 12 s at half, at most 20 s. Hits kill sailors (`COMPANY` in `gameplay/company.ts`): `damage ÷ max hull × crew × 0.6` for hull and waterline hits, `× 0.15` for rigging, and crashes count as hull hits; the fractions carry over, so many small hits add up. Crew only comes back by hiring (the tavern arrives with trade, 5c) or half of it after being sunk. The enemies' reload and the duel simulator ignore crew and level, so the balance tables are unchanged.
- **Boarding (`B`):** an enemy afloat at or below 40 % of its hull (or fleeing), alongside (centres within a quarter of the two lengths + 20 m) and moving within 4 m/s of you can be boarded; the prompt shows the party's odds (Lanchester's square law on crew × boarding bonus against the enemy's crew, which damage thins). The party is 90 % of your crew. The fight runs in rounds until one side is gone: a win costs the share of the party that died, takes gold (`price × 1000 × 8 % × 0.5…1.5 × loot bonus`) and one to three goods that fit in the hold, and scuttles the prize (it sinks as a kill with 1.5× experience); a defeat kills the whole party and blocks another try for 15 s. To catch a runner, match its course and speed. The unarmed Tartane can board too, but cannot cripple anything.
- **Levels:** sinking an enemy pays `strength × 0.1` experience (`strength` is √(hull × broadside), see `enemy.ts`; 1.5× when taken by boarding). Cumulative experience for level n is `100 × (n − 1)^1.6`, up to level 10. Each level above 1 shortens reloads by 2 %, raises loot by 5 % and boarding strength by 3 %.
- **Being sunk (`R`):** the cargo is lost, a quarter of the gold goes, at least half the crew is signed on again; experience, career kills and discoveries are kept. The enemy count (`GameView.kills`, per run) resets.

## Trade, the tavern and the shipyard (5c, 5d)
Moored, the harbour panel (tabs Pazar / Tersane / Taverna) sells and buys ten goods at prices that depend on the port (cheap where made, dear where wanted, with a 6 % spread and a small pressure that recovers), fills the hold up to `stats.hold`, and signs on sailors at 10 gold each. Rules, tables and targets are in `ECONOMY.md`; the panel sends `PortCommand`s through `Game.command`.
The Tersane tab lists the ships the port's yard builds with their stats, the part-exchange value of yours and a repair button (rules and prices in `ECONOMY.md`); buying swaps the player's ship at the quay, which is why `createGame` keeps the ship, its hull and the hold in a `GameState` and `GameView.ship.specId` tells `sailingMode` to draw another model. A moored hull mends slowly for nothing (0.2 %/s); the yard repairs at once for gold.
