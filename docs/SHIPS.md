# Ships (single source of truth)

16 period ships (17th–18th century Caribbean). Code: `src/ships/specs.ts` (data) and `src/ships/types.ts` (schema, units, field docs). This table mirrors the data; update both together. Tests (`tests/ships.test.ts`) guard the 16-ship set.

### Consistency notes (checked against the guide)
- The class number is a **tier**, not a physical size: Barque, Yacht and Lugger are all class 6.
- Stats are not monotonic in class; this is the original game's balance, kept as-is: Lugger (16) is as fast as a Corvette; Caravel has more cannons (32) than Brig or Pinnace (24) and more crew than Brig (240 vs 160); Galleon is joint-slowest (speed 7, with Tartane and Barque); in the guide's best Galleon yard its HP (5700) equals the Frigate's; Yacht carries 16 cannons; Tartane has none.
- Cannon count drives the number of visible gun ports; `gunDecks` is a visual-only row count.
- Gameplay balance may later be tuned independently of the original; keep this table as the reference.
- Copyright: the guide is © its author and the game data © the game's publisher. Fine for a prototype; derive our own numbers before any public release.

## Visual spec (see `types.ts`)
`length` is the station length of the hull; stem and transom lean make the overall mesh about 7% longer (e.g. Battleship 64 → ~68.5 m). Use the mesh bounds, not `length`, for docking or collision.

Ship local space: metres, +x bow, +y up, waterline y = 0. Hull is lofted from stations (stern → bow); `masts[].at` runs bow (0) → stern (1). Rigs: `square` (1–3 tiers, optional spanker), `lateen`, `gaff`, `lug`. Sketch reference style: dark hull with gold wale, cream sails, red pennant.

## Condition

`Ship.setCondition({ rigging, flooding, fire }, time)` (see `Condition` in `src/core/views.ts`) makes a model show its battering; it is called every frame and only mutates transforms and materials.

- **Rigging** 1 → 0: sail cloth lerps from cream to scorched grey-brown (per-ship material). On the procedural ship the sails shrink to 55% height, topsails vanish below 0.66, topgallants below 0.8 (further tiers below 0.88); courses and fore-and-aft sails always remain, and the height combines multiplicatively with `setSailTrim`. On a GLB ship (cloth shader, below) damage is a *tear*: the lower part of every tier is pulled up (more gathering) and the cloth flutters more; the tiers stay.
- **Flooding** 0 → 1: an inner `pivot` group (`root` is untouched) sinks up to 0.35 × draft and lists up to 0.18 rad to starboard, with a slow wobble.
- **Fire** 0 → 1: a pool of flame cones (mast bases, sterncastle, bow) shows more flames, taller, flickering as a pure function of `time`.
- `{ rigging: 1, flooding: 0, fire: 0 }` restores the pristine look. Smoke is a render particle effect, not part of the model.

## Sail and flag cloth (`clothState.ts`, `clothShader.ts`, `flagCloth.ts`, `trim.ts`)
`Ship.setSailTrim(windRel, level)` is called once a frame; `Ship.setWind?({ angle, speed, gust })` optionally gives the **apparent** wind at the ship (`angle` from the bow, + = from port; `speed` m/s; `gust` multiplier 0.75-1.25). Without `setWind` the cloth assumes a steady 8 m/s wind from `windRel`, so older callers keep working.

- **Flutter** is a shared baked field (`public/models/cloth/flutter.bin`, `tools/cloth/solver.ts`, `npm run bake:cloth`; see `docs/SHIP_ASSETS.md`), analytic until it has loaded.
- **State (CPU, pure, `clothState.ts`)**: per ship a few critically damped springs advanced on the wall clock (`clothClock`; exact integrators, so any `dt` is stable, and a call never counts more than 2 s): furl per tier slot (rate-limited, 0.55 of a tier per second), belly (square 0...1, fore-and-aft -1...1), flutter, yard brace and sheet angles, and the apparent wind as the flags see it. The first call snaps to the trim. **Furl order**: the lowest tier is gathered first (a tier starts when the one below is 45 % done), sail is set from the top tier down (a tier opens when the one above is below 55 %), and a lower tier is never more open than the one above it. Masts with fewer tiers than the ship's tallest use the upper slots.
- **Belly** follows the wind side through zero (a tack empties the sail and fills it on the other side, never a flip); square sails are never backed through the mast. **Flutter** rises in irons (apparent angle under about 50 degrees), in gusts, in a gale and with damage.
- **LOD0 sails (GPU)**: the pivot only swings (yards braced, fore-and-aft sails sheeted, eased by springs); the cloth shader (`onBeforeCompile` on the per-ship cloned sail and `SquareYards` materials, one shared GL program, a matching `customDepthMaterial` so the shadow follows; the procedural ship's unlit flags have no depth material, their shadow stays static) gathers each tier up to its **own** yard in ruched folds, scales the baked belly, adds travelling flutter and pins the edges. Yards stay put (on the yards mesh only footropes, boltropes and lifts below the head line move), so tiers never overlap and nothing sinks into the shrouds. Tier ranges, belly and head lines are derived **at load time** from the sail meshes (`clothGeometry.ts`; tests check all 16 GLBs), so no Blender regeneration is needed.
- **Every other sail uses the same shader**: LOD1/LOD2 run it with `uDetail = 0` (furl order, belly and tear identical, no flutter or folds), so a level switch does not pop; the procedural ships' sails, jibs and flags use it too. Jibs are hauled down their stay (scaled towards the tack), booms and gaffs follow their sail, and the corners of fore-and-aft sails shrink towards the head so none stays frozen. On the procedural ships the booms and yards are plain cylinders and stay put (only GLB booms follow their sail). Only a pivot whose sail is not the expected grid keeps the old scaled fallback in `trim.ts` (height `0.04 + 0.96 * (1 - mean furl)`, a mirror that follows the signed belly and never collapses, hidden below 5 %).
- **Flags** (`LOD0_Flag`, and the procedural pennant and stern ensign, now segmented strips): anchored at the pole, a wave travelling to the tip whose amplitude grows with `u` and with wind and gust, a sideways bend proportional to u^2 * sin(apparent angle) (the tip lines up with the wind), droop in light air and a tip flutter; the nation colour is unchanged. The pole/length/distance attributes are derived at load time from the mesh's strips (in the GLB they are separate flat quads and share one swatch UV, which is not used).
- **Cost**: vertex shader only; 165-1,990 vertices per mast; one cloth program per kind (sail, yards and jibs share one; flags, and the depth materials, have their own) shared by every ship; a handful of springs per ship; no per-frame allocation. Measured numbers are in `docs/SHIP_ASSETS.md`.
