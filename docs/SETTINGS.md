# Settings

`src/settings` (imports `core` only; `render`, `audio` and `ui` may import it) holds every option the player can change. The values are plain data (`schema.ts`: `Settings` = `graphics`, `audio`, `game`, `debug`), validated on load (`sanitize`: unknown keys dropped, bad types and out-of-range numbers fixed, never throws), saved as JSON under `localStorage['ship-rpg.settings']` (every access in try/catch; blocked storage just means nothing persists) and changed through the store (`getSettings()`: `get`, `patch(change, persist?)`, `preset(name)`, `reset`, `subscribe`).

- **Presets.** `PRESETS.low|medium|high` set the whole graphics group but `autoQuality` and `fpsLimit`; changing any single value away from every preset labels the group `custom` (`matchPreset`).
- **URL overrides** apply to the page load only and are never saved: `?quality=low|medium|high` (water quality, also stops the automatic step-down), `?aa=off|fxaa|msaa2|msaa4|msaa8`, `?debug` (overlay on). The old `K` key (cycles water quality) and the old `ship-rpg.quality` key still work.
- **Menu.** `ui/settings.ts` builds the menu from the declarative list in `ui/settingsControls.ts` (one entry per setting: select, range or toggle), so adding a setting is one entry there plus the code that reads it. The gear button (top right, both modes) and **Esc** open it; while it is open the sailing game is paused; the sailing menu has a "Galeriye dön" button. Controls take effect at once.
- **What reads what.** `app/sailingMode.ts` and `app/galleryMode.ts` subscribe to the store: water quality → `SailingView.setQuality`, audio group → `Audio.setVolumes` (master, effects, ambience; `muted` is the `N` key), game group → camera sensitivity and the page (`--hud-scale` zoom of the HUD, `.no-hints`), FPS limit → `main.ts` (skips drawing, the simulation keeps its fixed step). Automatic water step-down changes the value without saving it.
- Esc toggles the menu (not while held, not inside an open drop-down). 
- The automatic water step-down is switched off by `?quality=`, by a frame limit under 45 fps (a 30 fps cap looks slow by design) and once the player has changed the water quality himself (menu or `K`).
- `reset()` also drops URL overrides for the rest of the session.
- Tests: `tests/settings.test.ts`.

## Render pipeline (`render/pipeline.ts`)
Both views (sailing and gallery) draw through one pipeline instead of straight to the canvas: the renderer is created without the browser's own antialiasing, the scene goes into a (multisampled, half-float where `EXT_color_buffer_float` exists) render target, then **bloom** (HDR, before tone mapping), **tone mapping** (`OutputPass`: ACES or AgX with an exposure; every object, the sea and sky shaders included, is mapped together) and **FXAA**; the last pass writes the canvas. Settings: antialiasing (off, FXAA, MSAA 2x/4x/8x, capped at the GPU's `maxSamples`), render scale 50–200 % (the target's size relative to the canvas, which stays at the display's pixel ratio up to 2), bloom, tone mapping, exposure. When nothing in the chain is on, the frame is drawn straight to the canvas. `renderer.info` counts the whole frame (`autoReset` is off; the views reset it once per frame), so `?perf` includes the wake and post passes.

## Shadows, textures, particles, view distance
- **Shadows** (`render/shadows.ts`): one sun shadow map that follows the player's ship (in the gallery, the ship on show), covering `shadowRadius(ship length) × shadowDistance` (40–220 m): ships (hull, masts, sails), crew and the terrain they sail past cast and receive; the terrain only receives. The map's centre is snapped to whole texels along the light's axes so edges do not crawl. Quality: low 1024 (basic filter), medium 2048 (PCF), high 4096 (soft PCF). One map instead of cascades is enough because only the ships near the player matter; far islands do not shadow.
- **Ship shadows on the sea.** The sea is a custom shader and cannot read the shadow map, so each ship also paints a soft elliptical shadow (`shadowStamp`: along the sun's direction, as long as the ship's silhouette plus its masts' reach at the sun's height) into the **blue channel** of the wake map, which is redrawn every frame; the sea darkens there and loses its sun glitter. It follows the shadow setting (off when shadows are off) and works at every quality.
- **Textures** (`TEXTURE_QUALITY`): low/medium/high set the anisotropy (1/4/16, capped by the GPU), the sea's detail texture (128/256/512 px) and the sea-floor depth map (512×320 / 1024×640 / 2048×1280). Switching to high rebakes the depth map (about half a second).
- **Particles** thin out smoke, spray, debris and flames (muzzle flashes always show). **View distance** scales the fog.
- Debugging in dev: `__settings` (the store), `__env`, `__renderer`, `__scene` on `window`; `__env.wake.peaks(__renderer)` reads back the largest foam / ripple / shadow values in the wake map. Check the console for `WebGL: INVALID_OPERATION: useProgram: program not valid`: it means a shader failed to compile (one GLSL ES reserved word, `cast`, did exactly that).

## Global illumination and ambient occlusion
- **Sky light** (`render/gi.ts`, setting "Küresel aydınlatma" ≥ Gökyüzü ışığı): the live sky (hour, weather, clouds, sun) and the sea below the horizon are drawn into a 128 px cube map about every 1.5 s (at once when the sea state moves by 0.04), filtered with PMREM and set as `scene.environment`. Every standard material gets its ambient and reflected light from it: blue-white from above, sea-green from below, warm by the sun. The flat hemisphere light drops to 30 % and only fills in.
- **Ambient occlusion** (`render/pipeline.ts`): SSAO (cheap) or GTAO (better, with denoising) darken corners and contacts. They run on a second camera that skips layer 1 (the sea, sky, particles, name labels), so the sea's flat plane does not fake a contact shadow, within 500 m; the sun's shadow map is rendered once per frame even though the scene is drawn more than once (`shadowMap.autoUpdate` is off).
- **Screen-space bounce** (`render/ssgiPass.ts`, "Gökyüzü + ekran uzayı"): after GTAO, every pixel gathers the colour of the lit surfaces around it that face it (12 spiral samples, per-pixel rotation, weighted by facing and distance, using GTAO's depth and normals) and adds it, scaled by its own colour. It is experimental and the first thing to switch off when frames are slow; it brings GTAO with it (`effectiveOcclusion`).

## Debug overlay and profiler
`core/profiler.ts` keeps the last 120 frame times and per-section CPU timers (pure, injectable clock, tested): FPS, the 1 % low (mean of the slowest 1 % of the frames), average, p95 and worst frame, and the mean per-frame time of each section (`güncelleme`, `çizim` in `main.ts`). `ui/debugOverlay.ts` draws them (refreshed every 250 ms) with a 120-bar frame-time graph (green under 17.5 ms, amber under 25, red beyond), the JS heap, and the lines the current screen adds (`app/debugLines.ts`: draw calls and triangles, **GPU time** from `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` when the browser offers it, geometries/textures, resolution, MSAA, quality in force, enemies, ships, shots, crew, sea state, wind). **F3** toggles it, **Shift+F3** switches short/full, the "Hata ayıklama" tab of the menu does the same, `?debug` opens it for that page load. The idea comes from the frame panel of the `rpg-web-strategy` project.

## Day and night (`world/clock.ts`, `render/seaLook.ts`, `render/lamps.ts`)
- **The clock.** `hourAt(time, clock)`: the game time maps to an hour 0–24; a day lasts "Gün uzunluğu" minutes of play (default 24; menu: Oynanış), the game starts at 09:00, "Zamanı dondur" stops it. The hour is in `GameView.hour` (the HUD shows it as "Saat"), `Game.setClock({ dayMinutes, frozen, hour })` changes it, and the gallery runs its own day (starts 10:30). The menu's **Hava & Zaman** tab has an hour slider and quick buttons (Şafak, Sabah, Öğle, Gün batımı, Gece). Gameplay is unchanged by the hour (the balance tables assume fair weather and do not read it).
- **The sun and moon** (`sunState`): a northern latitude and a summer sun; the sun rises in the east, stands in the south at noon, sets in the west; the moon rides opposite, a little off. `daylight` (0 dark … 1 day) and `twilight` (the orange hours) drive everything else.
- **The look** (`lookFor(seaState, sun)`): the sky, horizon, fog, sea colours and hemisphere light blend between a day, a twilight and a night palette, and the storm palette is as dark as the hour. The main directional light is the sun while it is up and the moon after (blue, weaker), cross-fading at the horizon; it drives the shadows (ship shadows on the sea fade with its strength) and the sea's sun glitter. The sky shader draws the moon and a field of twinkling stars that clouds hide; the sky light probe refreshes when the hour moves.
- **Night lamps.** Materials named like `*glass*`, `window` or `lantern` (the GLB stern windows and lanterns, the procedural windows) glow in proportion to the dark. "Işık kalitesi" sets how many ships carry a real warm point light on their stern at night (low 0, medium 2, high 6): the nearest ones to the camera, so the count of lights in the shaders never changes.

## Weather simulator (menu: Hava & Zaman)
`world/weather.ts`: `weatherAt(time, overrides)` is the game's own weather (the wind of `windAt`, the sea state wind and storm cells build, rain and lightning that follow a rough sea, the sky as the sea state paints it) with any field the player takes over: wind speed and direction, sea state, cloud cover, haze, rain, lightning rate, and the four **wave tunings** (`SeaTuning`: height, wavelength, crest sharpness, speed). With nothing overridden it is exactly the weather the balance tables assume. `Game.setWeather(change)` (a field set to `undefined` goes back to the game, `null` gives all back) feeds the wind the ship sails by, the sea state that handles it, the buoyancy springs (`stepBody` takes the tuning), `GameView.weather` (cloud, fog, rain, lightning, tuning, the latest `strike`) and `game.dev.setSea`. The tuning reaches the sea shader as uniforms (`uLen`, `uSpeed`, and `uAmp`/`uChop` folded with it) and `waveHeight(…, tuning)` takes it on the CPU: `tests/waves.test.ts` evaluates the GLSL for tuned waves against the CPU surface, and `chop(amp, tuning)` keeps the surface from folding at any setting.
- **Rain** (`render/rain.ts`): 5000 streaks on a box that follows the camera, slanted with the wind and faded with distance, thinned by the particle setting, tinted by the haze colour. **Lightning:** the game rolls strikes (`weatherState.ts`, seeded, around the ship, every 6–22 s ÷ the lightning rate); a new strike flashes the sky and fill light and, after distance ÷ 340 s, thunder plays (`Audio.thunder`). **Cloud and haze** override the look's own cover and fog distances.
- **Panel** (`ui/weatherPanel.ts`): the hour slider and quick hours; weather presets (Durgun, Güzel gün, Bulutlu, Yağmurlu, Fırtına, Kasırga, Otomatik); a slider per field (Rüzgâr hızı/yönü, Deniz durumu, Dalga yüksekliği/boyu/keskinliği/hızı, Bulut, Sis, Yağmur, Şimşek) with an **Oto** button that gives that field back to the game; fields the player set are marked. The same panel works over the gallery (which has its own light breeze) and the sailing game. The simulator state is not saved: it lasts until you leave the screen.

## Music (`audio/musicPlan.ts`, `audio/music.ts`)
There is no recorded music (nothing to download or license): it is written on the spot. `planBar(mood, bar, rng)` is the score as pure data, from a mood `{ sea, day, docked }`: the **mode** (Lydian on a fair day, Ionian in harbour, Dorian at dusk and dawn, Aeolian at night, Phrygian in a storm), the **tempo** (slow at night and in harbour, restless in a storm), how many notes play (sparse bells at night, denser on a lively sea), a four-bar chord phrase, the bass, the plucks and bells, and low drums once the sea is rough. A seeded generator makes it the same piece every time; every note stays in the key of its mode (tested). `startMusic` plays the bars ahead of the clock (a 250 ms timer, 1.4 s of lookahead): a soft three-voice pad, a bass note, plucks and bells with an inharmonic partial, drums as sine thumps. `Audio.mood(...)` is called every frame from the sailing mode (sea state, daylight, docked); the "Müzik" slider (`audio.music`) sets the music bus, the mute key silences everything. The gallery has no sound.
